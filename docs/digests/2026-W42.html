PathTime-VLA: Path-Time Decoupling for Factorized Post-Training of Vision-Language-Action Policies

Qing Huang, Yifei Yang, Ziqing Zou et al.  ·  2026-10-08

Decouples trajectories into progress-indexed paths and monotone time laws for factorized VLA post-training and speed optimization.

ProblemStandard VLA action representations couple geometric routes with execution timing, inheriting sub-optimal operator pacing and teleoperation delays.ApproachRepresents motion as a progress-indexed path $X(s)$ and a time profile $t(s)$. Uses a staged procedure: DAgger for path priors, Speed-DQN for execution multipliers, and Path-AWR to refine diffusion path generation.ResultAchieved 58/60 task successes while reducing mean completion times by 39-52% compared to fixed-speed baselines.Why it mattersEnables independent optimization of path accuracy and execution speed, improving robot throughput and robustness to noisy teleoperation timing.

Beyond Actions: Learning Future Operation Targets for Vision-Language-Action Models

Min Lin, Bokai Zhou, Jianqi Lin et al.  ·  2026-10-09

Predicts learned 'Effect' tokens representing intended physical outcomes instead of raw action chunks for robot control.

ProblemVLA models typically map observations to action chunks without grounding them in resulting physical changes or future states. This lack of outcome-awareness limits performance in complex, coordinated bimanual dexterous manipulation.ApproachAFCE uses an offline tokenizer to map actions and visual transitions into discrete 'Effect' tokens. A flow-matching transformer predicts these tokens, which are then converted into executable action chunks by a state-conditioned decoder.ResultAchieved 50.97% success on DexJoCo, outperforming the pi_0.5 baseline by 11.82 and UVT by 15.21 percentage points. It showed significant gains in all five evaluated bimanual tasks.Why it mattersShifts VLA supervision from raw motor commands to outcome-oriented representations. This improves coordination and success in high-DOF tasks where precise action-to-outcome mapping is critical.

WARP-VLA: Wrist-Camera Adaptation for View-Robust Policy Execution in Vision-Language-Action Models

Junmyeong Lee, Dongmin Shin, Min-Gyu Park et al.  ·  2026-10-08

Robust VLA execution across diverse wrist-camera configurations using a Mixture-of-Experts architecture for implicit view adaptation.

ProblemVLA policies are highly sensitive to wrist-camera mounting variations that alter geometric cues during cross-setup deployment. Reproducing exact training camera poses is impractical for real-world robotics.ApproachWARP-VLA utilizes a Mixture-of-Experts architecture where specialized experts learn view-specific feature transformations. A router combines these experts based on implicit view information, eliminating the need for explicit camera extrinsic inputs.ResultImproves pi-0.5 success rate from 39.2% to 78.3% on the LIBERO benchmark under wrist-view perturbations. Demonstrated successful zero-shot transfer of feature-level adaptation to real-robot environments.Why it mattersProvides a plug-and-play solution for the common camera pose mismatch problem in robot deployment. Enables policies to generalize across different robot hardware mounting configurations without retraining.

Owen Du, Yang Yue, Jie Zhang et al.  ·  2026-10-06

Learns an action-consistent visual token pruning policy to accelerate frozen VLA models without sacrificing manipulation performance.

ProblemHigh computational overhead from redundant visual tokens limits real-time VLA deployment, while existing pruning methods lack action-specific optimization.ApproachTrains a lightweight pruning module using action-level supervision to minimize the discrepancy between actions from pruned and full visual contexts. Yuyan Li, Yujia Wang, Yusong Huang et al.  ·  2026-10-06

Dynamically determines action execution horizons by identifying divergence in imagined future states via an action-conditioned world model.

ProblemFixed-length or action-similarity-based chunking ignores that similar actions can diverge in state-space, leading to cascading errors or premature replanning.ApproachEmploys an action-conditioned world model to simulate trajectories for multiple candidate chunks. It applies Bayesian change-point inference to estimate the optimal execution horizon based on the consensus of imagined futures.ResultReduces failure rates by up to 71.8% across various benchmarks and real-robot tasks when applied to existing action-chunking policies.Why it mattersEnables robust, zero-shot deployment of VLA policies by using outcome-aware verification instead of heuristic action-space metrics to handle stochasticity.

Shangyuan Yuan, Xinda Qi, Yujiang Pu et al.  ·  2026-10-06

Jaemin Kim, Jiahn Kim, Taesik Gong  ·  2026-10-05

Accelerates zeroth-order VLA adaptation by reusing frozen prefix activations and implementing schedule-aware prefetching for faster gradient estimation.

ProblemFirst-order fine-tuning exceeds memory limits of deployment hardware, while standard zeroth-order optimization is prohibitively slow for large VLA models.ApproachThe method freezes the vision-language backbone and reuses its conditioning states across multiple perturbation queries. JitterVLA: A Replay Benchmark for Motion Quality in VLA-Driven Human-Robot Manipulation

Faith Ogunwoye, Didar Seyidov, Konstantin Gubernatorov et al.  ·  2026-10-09

JitterVLA is a replay benchmark for evaluating robot motion quality under sparse, delayed vision-language-action policy updates.

ProblemHigh-latency VLA inference produces sparse and irregular action targets, causing jerky, unsafe robot motion during human-robot interaction. Existing evaluations often fail to isolate controller-induced artifacts from policy-level errors.ApproachReplays identical VLA command streams and timing traces through different controllers to benchmark motion quality metrics like p99 jerk and tracking error. The framework evaluates interpolation and jerk-limited generation using synthetic data, human trajectories, and open VLA pipelines.ResultInterpolation reduced modeled p99 jerk by 83% (615 to 106 m/s^3) on human-driven trajectories with 200ms delay. Physical UR3 experiments confirmed a 3.0-14.0% reduction in accelerometer-derived jerk using interpolation techniques.Why it mattersProvides a standardized tool for ML engineers to optimize the low-level control interface of VLAs, improving safety and smoothness without retraining heavy models.

Evo-World: A Lightweight Vision-Language-Action Model with Future Motion Prediction via Optical-Flow Supervision

Zile Zhong, Yuxin Du, Tao Lin et al.  ·  2026-10-09

A 0.87B parameter VLA model using optical-flow supervision for future motion prediction to improve robot manipulation efficiency.

ProblemAction supervision alone lacks explicit scene anticipation, while future-image prediction objectives redundantly model static appearance instead of task-relevant motion. This leads to inefficient representation learning and high computational overhead for predictive capabilities.ApproachIntegrates optical-flow supervision as an auxiliary task to focus the model on future motion dynamics rather than pixel-level reconstruction. The training pipeline decouples representation learning from action optimization to preserve predictive features and regulate representation diversity.ResultAchieved 91.1% success on Meta-World MT50, a 13% relative improvement over non-predictive baselines, using only 0.87B parameters. Demonstrated superior real-world performance on AgileX robots with lower GPU memory usage and higher inference frequency.Why it mattersProves that motion-centric auxiliary tasks are more effective and lightweight than video generation for robot control. It provides a practical method for building real-time VLAs that understand scene evolution without generative overhead.
