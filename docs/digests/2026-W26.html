Co-VLA: Coordination-Aware Structured Action Modeling for Dual-Arm Vision-Language-Action Systems

Yandong Wang, Jiaqian Yu, Xiongfeng Peng et al.  ·  2026-06-18

Integrates structured action latents and latent-aware control into VLAs to enable explicit, interpretable dual-arm coordination.

ProblemImplicit coordination in monolithic VLA models fails in tightly coupled bimanual tasks, lacking the reliability, interpretability, and real-time controllability required for complex manipulation.ApproachImplements a Structured Action Expert that factorizes actions into shared intent and arm-specific residual latents using coordination-aware losses. A Latent-Aware Controller processes these latents to modulate synchronization, asymmetry, and safety constraints at the joint-command level.ResultYielded a 27% success rate improvement in tight-coordination tasks and doubled out-of-distribution performance while reducing task completion time by 25%.Why it mattersDemonstrates a modular architecture for injecting structural priors into VLA action heads, essential for scaling foundation models to complex, multi-agent or multi-arm robotic systems.

Thien-Loc Ha, Quang-Tan Nguyen, Trong-Bao Ho et al.  ·  2026-06-18

A framework for SO(2)-equivariant VLA models using equivariant perception and action heads for improved rotational generalization.

ProblemCurrent VLA models lack geometric inductive biases, causing them to struggle with rotational generalization and requiring excessive data for varied orientations.ApproachThe framework integrates EquiPerceptor, which extracts approximately SO(2)-equivariant representations from frozen ViT features, with EquiActor, an exactly SO(2)-equivariant Diffusion Transformer head. This setup creates an equivariance chain from image observations to flow-matching action sequences.ResultSuccess rates improved from 78.1% to 92.6% on LIBERO and from 54% to 72% on real-world Mobile ALOHA tasks.Why it mattersProvides a scalable method to inject geometric priors into frozen foundation models, significantly improving sample efficiency and spatial robustness in robot manipulation.

Does VLA Even Know the Basics? Measuring Commonsense and World Knowledge Retention in Vision-Language-Action Models

Nikita Kachaev, Andrey Moskalenko, Matvey Skripkin et al.  ·  2026-06-17

Evaluates world knowledge retention in VLAs using Act2Answer, a protocol mapping multiple-choice benchmarks to tabletop object-placement actions.

ProblemFine-tuning VLMs on robotics data may degrade world knowledge, yet failures often conflate cognitive gaps with low-level control errors. Current metrics cannot isolate semantic knowledge from motor execution performance.ApproachAct2Answer converts VLM benchmarks into tabletop episodes where agents select answers through pick-and-place actions, minimizing control complexity. Layerwise intent probing localizes answer-relevant semantic information across the VLM backbone and action head.ResultVLAs retain simple concepts but struggle with rich semantics compared to base VLMs, though VQA co-training significantly improves retention. Probing shows answer-relevant signals peak in middle layers and attenuate before reaching the action head.Why it mattersProvides a diagnostic framework to distinguish between reasoning failures and motor skill deficiencies in embodied agents. Essential for understanding how robotics fine-tuning affects the underlying semantic capabilities of VLA models.

Finetuning Vision-Language-Action Models Requires Fewer Layers Than You Think

Gia-Binh Nguyen, Trong-Bao Ho, Thien-Loc Ha et al.  ·  2026-06-18

Training-free layer pruning using CKA identifies and removes redundant VLA layers, halving depth while maintaining performance.

ProblemLarge-scale VLA architectures exhibit significant representational redundancy, causing prohibitive computational costs during downstream fine-tuning and high latency during real-time inference.ApproachUses a single-pass Centered Kernel Alignment analysis to identify and remove twin layers across the VLM backbone and control head. This training-free structural compression permanently reduces model depth by up to 50% before fine-tuning.ResultAchieved 40-50% faster training and 30% higher inference speed while matching or exceeding full-scale model performance on LIBERO, RoboCasa, and real-world benchmarks.Why it mattersProvides a zero-cost method to significantly lower the hardware requirements and latency for deploying and adapting multi-billion parameter foundation policies to new robotic tasks.

ProblemRapid wrist-camera motion and end-effector occlusions cause world models to lose scene persistence, leading to hallucinations during long-horizon rollouts. Motion-Focused Latent Action Enables Cross-Embodiment VLA Training from Human EgoVideos

Runze Xu, Yiluo Zhang, Jian Wang et al.  ·  2026-06-17

Extracts cross-embodiment action priors from unlabeled human egocentric videos using motion-decoupled latent VQ-VAE codebooks for VLA pre-training.

ProblemLarge-scale VLA training is bottlenecked by the scarcity of high-fidelity robotic action labels compared to abundant unlabeled human videos. Mapping human motion to diverse robotic embodiments without explicit action labels causes significant domain alignment challenges.ApproachA Hybrid Disentangled VQ-VAE uses physical masks to decouple motion from backgrounds, creating a universal latent action codebook. The VLM learns to predict these action intents, which are combined with features from a frozen visual encoder in an intent-perception decoupling architecture. This setup allows specialized action experts to handle embodiment-specific execution while the VLM provides general intent.ResultMatches performance of state-of-the-art VLA models despite using zero action labels during pre-training and only 50 fine-tuning trajectories. Evaluations across simulation and real-world tasks confirm effective cross-embodiment generalization from human data.Why it mattersEnables scaling robot learning by utilizing internet-scale human video datasets instead of limited robotic teleoperation data. The latent-action framework provides a robust path for cross-embodiment transfer and data-efficient fine-tuning.

Uncertainty Quantification for Flow-Based Vision-Language-Action Models

Ralf Römer, Maximilian Seeliger, Saida Liu et al.  ·  2026-06-16

Quantifies epistemic uncertainty in flow-based VLAs using velocity-field disagreement for failure detection and active multitask fine-tuning.

ProblemFlow-based VLAs lack mechanisms to quantify prediction confidence, leading to silent failures in out-of-distribution scenarios and inefficient data acquisition during adaptation.ApproachCalculates epistemic uncertainty by measuring velocity-field disagreement across a small ensemble of flow-matching heads. This metric drives SAVE, a framework that prioritizes high-uncertainty samples for active fine-tuning to minimize expert labels.ResultVFD uncertainty enables reliable failure detection on LIBERO and reduces required expert demonstrations for multitask adaptation by at least 22% compared to baselines.Why it mattersProvides a mathematically grounded method for safe deployment via failure awareness and a data-efficient strategy for fine-tuning large-scale robotic foundation models.
