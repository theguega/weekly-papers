Smooth Action Chunking Via Movement-Primitive Representations for Imitation Learning

Shota Nomura, Takayuki Osa, Yasuhiro Kato et al.  ·  2026-10-29

Integrates movement-primitive basis functions into action chunking to ensure smooth, continuous robot trajectories without requiring policy architecture retraining.

ProblemStandard action chunking creates jerky trajectories and boundary discontinuities that cause hardware vibration and velocity limit saturation. These temporal structures are only learned implicitly, leading to poor control quality on real robots.ApproachReplaces discrete action sequences with a linear combination of smooth basis functions to enforce trajectory smoothness by construction. Employs a closed-form inference-time correction to ensure successive chunks pass through the previous chunk's final commanded point.ResultReduces within-chunk jerk by 10x for ACT and 2.4x for DiT while maintaining task success. Boundary discontinuities are reduced by 73-84% with negligible computational overhead.Why it mattersEnables safer deployment of VLA and diffusion-based policies on physical hardware by eliminating mechanical instability. It provides a plug-and-play solution for any chunk-based imitation learning backbone.

PDRVLA: A Vision-Language-Action Model with Physical Dynamics Representations

Hanqing Wang, Zhaotian Cai, Ruichuan An et al.  ·  2026-10-27

Integrates physical dynamics into VLA models via future-feature prediction supervised by frozen world models without real-time video generation.

ProblemStandard VLA models lack physical grounding. Current video world model integrations introduce prohibitive latency for high-frequency robotic control loops.ApproachA lightweight Physical Dynamics Module extracts tokens from vision-language features to predict future RGB, depth, and optical-flow. Training utilizes auxiliary supervision from frozen video world models and incorporates embodied chain-of-thought and subtask annotations.ResultAchieved success rates of 99.3% on LIBERO, 89.9% on LIBERO-Plus, 93.6% on RoboTwin 2.0, and 81.5% on SimplerEnv.Why it mattersProvides a computationally efficient framework for grounding robot policies in physical dynamics using world-model distillation without inference-time generation overhead.

Lightweight RL safety mechanism adapting VLA action chunks for dynamic obstacle avoidance without fine-tuning the underlying model.

ProblemVLA models ignore spatial constraints and dynamic obstacles, while fine-tuning for safety is computationally expensive and degrades original task performance.ApproachTreats VLA output as trajectory references for a separate RL Stem policy. From 12 to 200 Demonstrations: How ACT, Diffusion Policy, SmolVLA and $π_{0.5}$ Scale on a Low-Cost Arm

Simon Cowling  ·  2026-10-27

Benchmarks ACT, Diffusion Policy, SmolVLA, and π0.5 scaling performance from 12 to 200 demonstrations on low-cost hardware.

ProblemDetermining the data-efficiency and performance scaling of modern imitation learning and VLA policies on budget robotic hardware. Specifically addresses the lack of comparative scaling data between scratch-trained and fine-tuned models.ApproachTests four policies across nested subsets of 12 to 200 demonstrations on a pick-and-place task with distractors. Compares LoRA against action-expert fine-tuning and decomposes failures into a four-stage execution ladder.ResultACT scales best to 92.5% success at 100 demonstrations, whereas π0.5 leads in low-data regimes (40% at 12). SmolVLA and Diffusion Policy underperformed due to 'failure to start' errors and sensitivity to demonstration idle time.Why it mattersProvides empirical evidence that fine-tuning strategy (action-expert vs. LoRA) and dataset cleaning (lead-in trimming) are as critical as model architecture for VLA deployment.

JevVLA: Jev-Inspired Energy-Based Adaptation for Vision-Language-Action Policies

Wei Wang, Wenqiao Zhang, Zheqi Lv et al.  ·  2026-10-26

A lightweight energy-based adaptation framework that unifies discrete selection and continuous action guidance for frozen VLA policies.

ProblemAdapting pretrained VLAs to improve both discrete candidate selection and continuous motion control using a single, shared mechanism. Most current methods handle these modalities separately or require extensive fine-tuning of the entire base policy.ApproachA differentiable scalar energy function is learned on top of frozen VLA representations using execution feedback or demonstrations. This energy model ranks discrete candidates and uses action gradients to iteratively refine continuous action chunks.ResultSuccess rates increased by 1.28 to 2.31 percentage points across MetaWorld MT50, VIMA, LanguageTable, LIBERO, and CLIPort. Gains were achieved using only 80,000 trainable parameters while maintaining the frozen base policy.Why it mattersProvides a parameter-efficient strategy to adapt large-scale VLA foundation models to downstream tasks with heterogeneous action spaces. It demonstrates how energy-based gradients can serve as a universal refinement layer for diverse robotic control tasks.

Sooyoung Kwon, Carlee Joe-Wong, Kyunghan Lee  ·  2026-10-25

Optimizes VLA inference by dynamically switching between token reuse and pruning based on measured token drift.

ProblemHigh prefill latency on embedded hardware caused by processing hundreds of visual tokens at every control step.ApproachTRIAGE monitors token drift in shallow layers to decide between recomputation, KV-cache reuse, or pruning. It prioritizes a fixed budget for recomputing important tokens while reusing valid caches and pruning stale data.ResultReduced prefill latency on Jetson AGX Orin by 48% while maintaining success rates within 3% of full recomputation.Why it mattersEnables real-time deployment of large VLA models on untethered mobile robots and humanoids without requiring expensive retraining.

Minsu Jang, Jae-Woo Choi, Youngwoo Yoon et al.  ·  2026-10-30

Evaluates VLA policies' failure to remain stationary under null or infeasible instructions and tests three motion suppression strategies.

ProblemVLA policies exhibit unintended task execution and movement when provided with missing, nonsensical, or infeasible natural language instructions.ApproachEvaluated six VLA checkpoints using a paired protocol of valid and invalid instructions. SACQ: Structure-Aware Codebook Quantization for Ultra-Low-Bit Vision-Language-Action Models

Haesung Lee, Insu Han  ·  2026-10-30

Ultra-low-bit vector quantization framework using action-aware metrics and structure-aware codebook fitting for efficient VLA deployment.

ProblemStandard low-bit post-training quantization degrades VLA performance by ignoring downstream operation structures and the sensitivity of robotic actions to weight errors.ApproachUses structure-aware codebook fitting with role-specific quadratic metrics and action-aware mixed-bit allocation. A two-stage refinement reassigns codeword indices with second-order compensation and calibrates codebooks against decoded actions.ResultMaintains 93.85% success on LIBERO at 1.5 bits per weight, offering 4.83x memory reduction and 1.51x speedup over BF16.Why it mattersEnables high-performance VLA deployment on resource-constrained robotic platforms by minimizing the performance gap between compressed and full-precision policies.

Xin Ma, Furong Chen, Xinlong Man et al.  ·  2026-10-29

A multidimensional evaluation framework for VLA models covering capability, affordability, and reproduction evidence via standardized 0-100 profiles.

ProblemSuccess rates alone ignore practical constraints like compute budgets, deployment requirements, and the reliability of research claims. Heterogeneous evaluation protocols prevent objective comparisons across diverse robot learning models.ApproachThe framework quantifies performance across five dimensions using fixed normalization anchors and weighted input coverage. It integrates local training, weight evaluation, and task-level audits to produce standardized profiles for training-constrained and deployment-constrained settings.ResultAssessments reveal that optimal backbone selection is budget-dependent and highly sensitive to specific adaptation recipes. The study identifies critical evidence gaps and interface-driven performance differences between language and visual task execution.Why it mattersEnables engineers to select models based on hardware-specific affordability rather than just raw performance. It offers a verifiable methodology for auditing model claims and estimating downstream fine-tuning costs.
