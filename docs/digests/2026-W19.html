LaST-R1: Reinforcing Action via Adaptive Physical Latent Reasoning for VLA Models

Hao Chen, Jiaming Liu, Zhonghao Yan et al.  ·  2026-04-30

Integrates adaptive latent physical reasoning with reinforcement learning to jointly optimize world modeling and action generation in VLAs.

ProblemExisting VLA reasoning is confined to static imitation learning or optimizes action spaces in isolation, bypassing the underlying physical reasoning process.ApproachIntroduces Latent-to-Action Policy Optimization (LAPO) to jointly refine latent Chain-of-Thought reasoning and action execution. An adaptive mechanism dynamically adjusts the reasoning horizon based on environment complexity.ResultAchieved a 99.8% success rate on LIBERO and a 44% improvement in real-world task performance over supervised warm-up policies.Why it mattersDemonstrates a methodology for transitioning robotic reasoning from imitation to self-improving RL, enhancing physical dynamics modeling for complex manipulation.

Embodied Interpretability: Linking Causal Understanding to Generalization in Vision-Language-Action Models

Hanxin Zhang, Mingshuo Xu, Abdulqader Dhafer et al.  ·  2026-05-01

Introduces interventional metrics ISS and NMR to quantify causal misalignment and predict generalization in VLA policies.

ProblemVLA policies often fail under distribution shift because they rely on spurious visual correlations rather than task-relevant causal factors.ApproachUses an interventional masking procedure called Interventional Significance Score (ISS) to estimate the causal influence of visual regions. Defines Nuisance Mass Ratio (NMR) to measure model attribution directed at task-irrelevant features.ResultISS provides more faithful explanations than gradient-based methods, and the NMR metric reliably predicts policy generalization across diverse manipulation tasks.Why it mattersEnables engineers to diagnose if a model is attending to spurious cues, providing a principled metric for evaluating policy robustness and failure modes.

CF-VLA: Efficient Coarse-to-Fine Action Generation for Vision-Language-Action Policies

Fan Du, Feng Yan, Jianxiong Wu et al.  ·  2026-04-27

A two-stage coarse-to-fine framework replacing iterative flow-based sampling with structured initialization and single-step refinement for efficient VLA policies.

ProblemFlow-based VLA policies require computationally expensive multi-step inference to recover action structure from Gaussian noise, limiting real-time robot control.ApproachThe model utilizes a coarse stage to transform Gaussian noise into an action-aware starting point via endpoint velocity prediction. A fine stage then performs a single-step refinement from this initialization using a stepwise training strategy and joint optimization.ResultReduces action sampling latency by 75.4% while achieving an 83.0% real-robot success rate, outperforming the NFE=10 baseline by 4.0 points.Why it mattersProvides a high-performance method to deploy expressive generative action heads on real-time hardware by significantly reducing inference-time function evaluations.

Kaijun Zhou, Qiwei Chen, Da Peng et al.  ·  2026-04-27

Systematic benchmarking and optimization of VLA models across heterogeneous edge accelerators for efficient on-robot deployment.

ProblemReal-time VLA inference is bottlenecked by hardware underutilization caused by alternating compute-bound VLM backbones and memory-bound Action Expert phases.ApproachIntroduces DP-Cache to reduce diffusion redundancy and V-AEFusion to enable asynchronous pipeline parallelism between VLM and action components. Evaluates models using a Cost-Energy-Time (CET) framework across diverse XPUs.ResultAchieved up to 2.9x speedup on desktop GPUs and 6x speedup on edge NPUs with marginal task success degradation.Why it mattersProvides hardware-aware optimization techniques and a cross-accelerator leaderboard essential for deploying large VLAs on power-constrained robotic platforms.

Qi Li, Bo Yin, Weiqi Huang et al.  ·  2026-04-26

A unified taxonomy and survey of safety threats, defenses, and evaluation protocols for embodied Vision-Language-Action models.

ProblemVLA models face unique safety risks including physical irreversibility, multimodal attack surfaces, and error propagation that existing LLM or classical robotics safety frameworks fail to address.ApproachThe survey categorizes safety across attack and defense timing axes, covering training-time threats like data poisoning and inference-time issues like cross-modal adversarial patches. It maps vulnerabilities through the lenses of attacks, defenses, evaluation benchmarks, and deployment domains.ResultIdentifies critical gaps in the field, specifically the lack of certified robustness for physical trajectories and the absence of standardized safety-aware training protocols.Why it mattersProvides a technical roadmap for securing robot control loops against adversarial inputs and training-time vulnerabilities, essential for deploying VLA models in high-stakes environments.

STARRY: Spatial-Temporal Action-Centric World Modeling for Robotic Manipulation

Yuxuan Tian, Yurun Jin, Bin Yu et al.  ·  2026-04-29

Unified diffusion-based policy jointly denoising spatial-temporal latents and actions with geometry-aware attention modulation for precise robotic manipulation.

ProblemWeak coupling between predictive representations and action execution in VLAs leads to failures in tasks requiring precise 3D spatial-temporal coordination.ApproachJointly denoises future spatial-temporal latents and actions within a single diffusion process. Incorporates Geometry-Aware Selective Attention Modulation (GASAM) to integrate predicted depth and end-effector geometry into token-aligned attention weights.ResultAchieved over 93% success on 50 bimanual tasks in RoboTwin 2.0 and increased real-world success rates from 42.5% to 70.8% compared to pi_0.5.Why it mattersProvides a robust framework for coupling world modeling with action generation, addressing the 2D-to-3D gap in VLA policies for complex manipulation.

KinDER: A Physical Reasoning Benchmark for Robot Learning and Planning

Yixuan Huang, Bowen Li, Vaibhav Saxena et al.  ·  2026-04-28

A benchmark for evaluating isolated kinematic and dynamic physical reasoning in robot learning through 25 procedurally generated tasks.

ProblemCurrent robot learning benchmarks often conflate physical reasoning with perception and language processing. This makes it difficult to isolate failures in kinematic logic, tool use, and dynamic constraint satisfaction.ApproachKinDER provides 25 environments across five categories: spatial relations, nonprehensile manipulation, tool use, geometric constraints, and dynamic constraints. It includes a Gymnasium-compatible API, demonstrations, and 13 baselines spanning TAMP, RL, IL, and foundation models.ResultEvaluation of 13 baselines reveals significant performance gaps in physical reasoning despite provided skills and demonstrations. Real-to-sim-to-real experiments on a mobile manipulator confirm the benchmark's physical validity.Why it mattersVLA models frequently fail at low-level physical grounding; this benchmark provides isolated environments to test and improve spatial and dynamic reasoning capabilities.

Thinking in Text and Images: Interleaved Vision--Language Reasoning Traces for Long-Horizon Robot Manipulation

Jinkun Liu, Haohan Chi, Lingfeng Zhang et al.  ·  2026-05-01

Interleaved Vision-Language Reasoning traces provide explicit causal and geometric grounding for long-horizon manipulation tasks.

ProblemLong-horizon tasks require both logical causal ordering and geometric grounding, which single-modality or latent-only plans fail to capture.ApproachA multimodal transformer generates global 'traces' of alternating textual subgoals and visual keyframes before conditioning a closed-loop action decoder. Pseudo-supervision for traces is produced by temporally segmenting and captioning existing demonstration datasets.ResultAchieved 92.4% success on LIBERO-Long, significantly outperforming text-only (62.0%) and vision-only (68.4%) trace alternatives.Why it mattersDemonstrates that explicit multimodal intermediate representations are superior to latent planning for scaling VLA models to complex, multi-stage robotic tasks.

Libra-VLA: Achieving Learning Equilibrium via Asynchronous Coarse-to-Fine Dual-System

Yifei Wei, Linqing Zhong, Yi Liu et al.  ·  2026-04-27

Decouples robotic manipulation into a coarse-to-fine hierarchy using a discrete Semantic Planner and continuous Action Refiner.

ProblemMonolithic VLA models suffer from a semantic-actuation gap when mapping high-level instructions directly to high-frequency continuous motor commands.ApproachA dual-system architecture utilizes a discrete Semantic Planner for macro-intent and a continuous Action Refiner for micro-pose alignment.
