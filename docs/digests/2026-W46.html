TactileLab: Sim-to-Real Dexterous Robot Manipulation with Efficient Shear-Sensitive Tactile Simulation

Yijiong Lin, Wenhao He, Toby Jack et al.  ·  2026-11-05

A scalable tactile simulation framework using rigid-body dynamics to estimate shear flow for efficient sim-to-real dexterous manipulation training.

ProblemHigh-fidelity soft-body simulation of tactile shear is too computationally expensive for large-scale RL, limiting the development of contact-rich manipulation. Existing methods also struggle with the sensor-specific domain gap during sim-to-real transfer.ApproachTactileLab uses rigid-body physics to efficiently approximate dense tangential motion at contact interfaces, generating simulated tactile flow fields and depth maps. It employs a real-to-sim translation module to map physical tactile camera streams into these standardized simulated representations.ResultThe method enables successful sim-to-real transfer for dual-arm and dexterous in-hand manipulation tasks across heterogeneous sensor designs. Policies exhibit emergent contact-aware behaviors and high training efficiency compared to traditional soft-body simulation approaches.Why it mattersProvides a high-throughput pipeline for incorporating shear-sensitive tactile feedback into robot learning, facilitating the development of dexterous VLA models that require large-scale simulation.

Localizing and Editing the Goal Representation of a Vision-Language-Action Model

Sudhanshu Srivastava, Faisal Aqlan  ·  2026-11-06

Localizes goal representations in OpenVLA using activation patching and steering to analyze and correct failure to follow prohibitions.

ProblemOpenVLA fails to process linguistic negation, executing forbidden tasks in 79% of cases. It is unclear where goal information is represented or how to effectively edit it for safety.ApproachEmploys causal mediation analysis through activation patching, linear probing, and steering vectors on the LIBERO-Goal benchmark. The study maps goal influence across instruction and action tokens from early to late transformer layers.ResultNegation had no effect on execution, but fixed steering at layer 8 redirected 66% of rollouts. Layer-32 goal read-outs proved more effective at detecting non-compliance than standard failure detectors.Why it mattersProvides evidence that safety constraints in VLAs require early-layer intervention or external monitoring rather than simple prompt-based negation. Offers specific layer targets for model editing and auditing.

Should the Action Expert Train the VLM? Action-Token Supervision and Gradient Routing in Mixture-of-Transformer VLAs

Asher James Hancock, Boyi Li, Yuxiao Chen et al.  ·  2026-11-03

Evaluates gradient coupling versus insulation in VLAs, proving that backpropagating action gradients significantly improves closed-loop robot control.

ProblemStandard VLA recipes block action-expert gradients to protect pre-trained VLMs, potentially preventing the learning of features necessary for control.ApproachDisentangles action-token supervision from gradient routing by comparing insulated versus coupled training of flow-matching experts and VLM backbones. Experiments evaluate performance across open-loop and closed-loop driving and robot manipulation tasks.ResultGradient coupling improved closed-loop driving progress by 17% with action-token supervision and 40% without it, while consistently boosting manipulation success.Why it mattersChallenges the standard 'insulation' training recipe, demonstrating that end-to-end gradient flow is essential for high-performance, closed-loop robotic VLA deployments.

LoopQ: Fair, Closed-Loop Evaluation of Quantized Vision-Language-Action Models

Syed Umer Bukhari, Magnus Almgren, Ahmed Ali-Eldin  ·  2026-11-03

A standardized closed-loop benchmark evaluating VLA post-training quantization methods across diverse hardware and robotic tasks.

ProblemVLA quantization research lacks fair, head-to-head comparisons, often using inadequate benchmarks that hide performance degradation and overestimating inference speedups.ApproachEstablishes a unified evaluation framework comparing five PTQ methods (RTN, AWQ, GPTQ, ActQuant, QuantVLA) across four VLA architectures. It utilizes 180,000 LIBERO/LIBERO-plus episodes and benchmarks latency on H100, L4, and Jetson AGX Thor.ResultSimple baselines like RTN and AWQ match or outperform VLA-specific methods at 4-bits, while LIBERO-plus reveals success rate drops of 1.5-4.3 points missed by standard LIBERO. Quantization yields 2-3x memory savings but negligible speedups because compute-bound backbones and unquantized action experts dominate latency.Why it mattersProvides evidence that complex VLA-specific quantization is often unnecessary compared to standard PTQ and identifies critical hardware bottlenecks in quantized robot inference.

ActPrune: Action-Grounded Visual Token Pruning for Efficient Vision-Language-Action Models

Sungwon Moon, Minji Kim, Hyemin Boo et al.  ·  2026-11-06

Training-free visual token pruning using action-grounded geometric priors to accelerate VLA model inference before visual encoding begins.

ProblemHigh visual token counts in VLAs cause inference latency that hinders real-time closed-loop control. Existing pruning techniques operate post-encoding, leaving the vision backbone's computational bottleneck unaddressed.ApproachLeverages the previous action chunk to predict token importance before encoding. It preserves third-person tokens along projected trajectories and prunes wrist-camera tokens that remain static after motion compensation.ResultDelivers up to 1.61x inference speedup and 59.5% FLOPs reduction with negligible success rate loss on LIBERO, VLABench, and real robots.Why it mattersProvides a zero-shot efficiency boost for large-scale VLA models. Engineers can deploy computationally heavy models at higher control frequencies without the need for expensive fine-tuning.

VICTR: Value-Informed In-Context Learning for Vision-Language-Action Models

Luca Macesanu, Tianluo Zhang, Aditya Patil et al.  ·  2026-11-05

Retrieves context for VLA in-context learning using a progress-informed value function estimator instead of visual similarity.

ProblemRetrieval-based in-context learning in VLAs often fails because visual similarity does not capture the specific execution stage required for action conditioning.ApproachIntegrates an In-Context Value Function Estimator (IC-VFE) that aligns current observations with reference demonstrations to estimate task progress. This progress estimate selects temporally relevant demonstration chunks to condition a policy post-trained for in-context action generation.ResultIncreased real-world out-of-domain success rates from 6.2% to 23.8% and improved task progress from 28.9% to 55.5%.Why it mattersProvides a scalable method for few-shot adaptation of large robot models to novel tasks by leveraging semantic temporal alignment over raw visual similarity.

A multi-embodiment VLA foundation model using three-stage training and rollback-based CFG-DAgger for robust desktop manipulation.
