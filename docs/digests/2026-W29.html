CAC-VLA: Context-Gated Action Conditioning for Vision-Language-Action Models

Yifu Xiong, Wenhao Yu, Jiaxuan Lin et al.  ·  2026-07-06

Integrates context-gated latent-action prediction within VLMs to bridge the gap between multimodal reasoning and precise motor control.

ProblemStandard VLM representations are not optimized for action conditioning, forcing the action expert to bridge multimodal understanding and motor control without explicit action-level guidance.ApproachLearns a lightweight interface within the VLM to predict coarse-to-fine latent actions encoded from future trajectories. These latent representations are adaptively fused with VLM features via a context gate to condition the downstream action expert.ResultAchieved a 98.3% success rate on LIBERO and 89.5% on LIBERO-Plus, demonstrating superior performance in continuous robot manipulation tasks.Why it mattersProvides a scalable way to embed action-space awareness directly into VLM backbones, improving the efficiency of conditioning for continuous action experts.

SIEVE: Structure-Aware Data Selection for Imitation Learning with VLA Models

Changti Wu, Bin Yu, Zhaolong Shen et al.  ·  2026-07-07

SIEVE selects high-value demonstrations by decomposing trajectories into reusable visuo-motor primitives to maximize structural exposure.

ProblemStandard VLA data selection ignores the compositional structure of long-horizon tasks, leading to redundancy and poor coverage of critical sub-behaviors.ApproachTrajectories are segmented into visuo-motor primitives and transition interfaces. Selection budgets are allocated to unique composition patterns via submodular optimization, followed by medoid-based filtering to retain stable demonstrations.ResultSIEVE surpasses full-data training performance while using only 50% of demonstrations and 50% of training iterations.Why it mattersProvides a scalable framework to reduce VLA training costs and improve policy robustness by prioritizing structural diversity over raw dataset size.

A dual-system model combining a vision-language subtask planner with a world-aware action chunking executor for robot manipulation.

ProblemWorld Action Models lack high-level planning for multi-step tasks, and fair performance comparisons between WAMs and VLAs are currently unavailable.ApproachSystem 2 vision-language planner decomposes goals into subtasks, while a System 1 WAM executor predicts action chunks via video-action co-training. Xinchuan Qiu, Yi Yu  ·  2026-07-06

A curriculum-based demonstration organization strategy for VLA models using task decomposition and progressive complexity.

ProblemConventional end-to-end robotic imitation learning lacks data structure, leading to training instability and poor generalization in long-horizon manipulation. Most research ignores how demonstration data is sequenced and organized during training.ApproachDecomposes complex tasks into atomic sub-skills and standardizes environments to minimize variability. CLAP: Direct VLM-to-VLA Adaptation via Language-Action Grounding

Yuri Ishitoya, Jeremy Siburian, Masashi Hamaya et al.  ·  2026-07-09

Adapts VLMs to VLAs by prepending numeric action tokens with natural-language descriptions to maintain pretrained distribution alignment.

ProblemPredicting numeric actions directly shifts generation away from the VLM's pretrained language distribution, degrading semantic capabilities and hindering transfer. This makes it difficult to isolate the VLM's contribution to robotic control.ApproachCausal Language-Action Prediction (CLAP) prepends natural-language action descriptions to numeric sequences, causally conditioning actions on a language-based plan. This method uses single-epoch fine-tuning without modifying the backbone architecture or introducing heavy post-training.ResultThe 2B CLAP model achieved 90.8% on LIBERO, a 14.9 point improvement over VLA-0, and demonstrated superior robustness under spatial and object perturbations.Why it mattersOffers a lightweight, backbone-preserving method to convert VLMs into VLAs while maintaining semantic reasoning and enabling controlled scaling analysis.

Training-Free Acceleration for Vision-Language-Action Models with Action Caching and Refinement

Ryuji Oi, Hikari Otsuka, Kosuke Matsushima et al.  ·  2026-07-07

Accelerates flow-based VLA inference by warm-starting iterative denoising via a retrieval-based action cache of previous intermediate trajectories.

ProblemIterative denoising in flow-matching action heads creates high inference latency, preventing real-time robot control. Current models struggle with the trade-off between sampling steps and task success.ApproachActionCache stores intermediate actions indexed by compact multimodal keys for cross-episode retrieval. It uses retrieved actions to warm-start flow-matching near target distributions, reducing required refinement steps without additional training.ResultAchieved inference acceleration of 11.75x on pi_0.5 and 34.43x on GR00T-N1.6 while preserving high task success rates in simulation and real-world trials.Why it mattersEnables real-time deployment of heavy flow-based VLA models on hardware with limited compute without retraining. This bridges the gap between high-fidelity flow policies and low-latency control.

Diagnosing Semantic Handoff Failures in Agent-Orchestrated Vision-Language-Action Skill Composition

Ke Rui, Yushen Zuo, Jiawei Wang et al.  ·  2026-07-07

Evaluates long-horizon VLA skill composition failures caused by distribution shifts between clean demonstrations and messy chained execution states.

ProblemIndividual VLA skills often meet their postconditions while leaving the robot or environment in states that prevent subsequent skills from starting.ApproachAn execution harness in BEHAVIOR-1K compares $π_{0.5}$ skill checkpoints across clean-snapshot and chained-state distributions. The system utilizes multi-view VLM verification for transition logic, retries, and replanning during long-horizon tasks.ResultSkills achieve 77-100% success from clean snapshots, yet frequently fail during composed rollouts due to poor next-skill readiness and grounding.Why it mattersDemonstrates that high single-skill benchmark performance is insufficient for long-horizon tasks; models must specifically learn robustness to chained-state distributions.
