Robot Learning with Vision-Language-Action Models for Agricultural Manipulation

Hamid Hafezi, Javad Mohammadpour Velni, Mohammadreza Davoodi  ·  2026-10-19

Adapts Octo and pi_0.5 VLA models to agricultural manipulation using a multi-stage sim-to-real fine-tuning pipeline.

ProblemAdapting generalist VLA policies to agricultural tasks is hindered by complex plant geometries and high data collection costs. Real-world demonstrations for unstructured biological environments are expensive and difficult to scale.ApproachA MuJoCo-based tomato harvesting environment generates expert demonstrations for initial adaptation of Octo and pi_0.5 models. These policies are subsequently fine-tuned on a physical Franka manipulator using real-robot demonstrations to mitigate sim-to-real gaps.ResultThe pi_0.5 model outperforms Octo, showing higher task success and superior robustness to viewpoint shifts and out-of-distribution plant structures. Findings indicate that pretrained policies benefit from hierarchical simulation and real-world fine-tuning in agricultural domains.Why it mattersDemonstrates practical deployment of VLA models in unstructured, non-industrial environments using a scalable sim-to-real transfer methodology.

Learn the Task, Not the Schedule: Reducing Spurious Cross-Arm Coupling in Bimanual Imitation Learning

Shuyuan Hu, Deng-Ke Wu, Yifan Jin et al.  ·  2026-10-19

Reduces spurious bimanual coupling by training on constraint-preserving temporal recombinations of arm-local sensorimotor trajectories.

ProblemBimanual imitation learning models exploit incidental timing schedules as shortcut cues, causing robots to stall if one arm encounters unexpected delays.ApproachConstraint-preserving Temporal Recombination (CTR) retimes recorded arm trajectories to generate diverse, admissible schedules during training. This breaks spurious temporal correlations while maintaining necessary task-based cross-arm constraints.ResultCTR increased worst-state success from 56% to 76% and reduced delay transfer between arms from 2.56s to 0.25s. Task constraint violations dropped from 36-98% to 0-4%.Why it mattersProvides a data-driven method to decouple bimanual control, making VLA models robust to temporal variations and preventing execution failures caused by inter-arm dependencies.

Johannes Keller, Albrecht M. Bloße, Adrian Lindenmeyer et al.  ·  2026-10-21

Estimates VLA epistemic uncertainty using post-hoc kernel-variance heads on single-model embeddings without retraining or ensembles.

ProblemUncertainty estimation in large VLA models typically requires ensembles, which are computationally expensive to train, store, and execute in real-time.ApproachImplements non-parametric kernel-variance heads that compute Gaussian process conditional variance from a frozen model's embeddings relative to training data. What Would Removing This Corruption Change? Failure Attribution for Vision-Language-Action Models

Carson Sobolewski, Anjali Parashar, Fei Chen et al.  ·  2026-10-19

Estimates visual corruption impact on VLA failure through a factorized risk model and latent intervention without counterfactual rollouts.

ProblemIdentifying which specific visual corruptions cause VLA failures in multi-corruption scenarios is difficult without expensive, paired counterfactual rollouts. Simple corruption detection fails because presence does not always correlate with policy failure risk.ApproachLearns a factorized risk model with decoupled representations for scene content and individual corruptions. It performs latent interventions to estimate the change in failure probability if a specific corruption were removed, requiring only the current image and task instruction.ResultOutperforms monolithic baselines and frontier VLMs, achieving 3x lower attribution error on OpenVLA and pi-0.5 across simulation and real-robot experiments. The model successfully adapts to varying corruption intensities and execution contexts for online monitoring.Why it mattersEnables precise diagnosis of VLA failures in noisy environments, facilitating targeted system corrections and robust online safety monitoring. Critical for deploying foundation models in real-world robotics where visual conditions are unpredictable.

Zhe Liu, Yuxuan Zheng, Jinzhe Qin et al.  ·  2026-10-18

An asynchronous VLA framework using B-spline trajectories and execution-aware RL for continuous, low-latency language-conditioned UAV control.

ProblemOnboard VLA inference latency causes temporal mismatches between predicted trajectories and the robot's real-time state, disrupting continuous flight.ApproachGenerates continuous B-spline trajectories and uses a fusion mechanism to maintain derivative continuity during asynchronous updates. Employs Execution-aware Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to fine-tune the policy against execution-induced deviations using trajectory-level rewards.ResultAchieved a 76.56% success rate on UAV-Flow, outperforming OpenVLA-UAV by 38.1% while significantly reducing acceleration and jerk.Why it mattersDemonstrates a robust method for deploying computationally expensive VLA models on high-dynamics hardware by bridging the gap between inference time and real-time execution.

Progress Probes $P^2$: Lightweight Runtime Monitoring for Vision-Language-Action Models

Atiksh Bhardwaj, Edward W. Duan, Junwon Seo et al.  ·  2026-10-22

Monitors VLA task progress by training linear probes on internal activations to detect runtime failures without external models.

ProblemVLAs suffer from unpredictable failures, yet existing runtime monitoring solutions require computationally expensive external foundation models. This overhead limits real-time deployment and safety of autonomous robotic policies.ApproachTrains linear probes on internal VLA activations from successful demonstrations to predict normalized task progress. Failures are detected at runtime by identifying significant deviations from the expected progress trajectory without requiring explicit failure data.ResultOn the π0.5 VLA model, Progress Probes outperform or match VLM-based monitors and supervised failure classifiers in task failure detection. The approach provides a lightweight, internal alternative to external supervisors across simulated and real manipulation tasks.Why it mattersShows that VLA internal representations implicitly capture task progress, enabling high-frequency failure detection with minimal computational overhead. This reduces the need for expensive secondary foundation models for robot monitoring.

Distills 3D geometric priors into temporal memory tokens via flow-matching for geometry-aware VLA control without runtime 3D reconstruction.

ProblemVLA models lack persistent 3D scene understanding, often relying on transient 2D observations or discarding inferred geometry instead of accumulating task-relevant spatial memory.ApproachUses a frozen geometry teacher to supervise GeoPlan tokens via flow-matching on point maps and future trajectories. These tokens are maintained in a temporal memory that adaptively fuses historical and current representations for inference-time action generation.ResultReached 98.9% success on LIBERO and 78.0% on real-world tasks, demonstrating high robustness in both clean and randomized environments.Why it mattersProvides a framework for embedding 3D structural awareness into VLAs while maintaining inference efficiency by avoiding explicit point-cloud processing or 3D teacher invocation.
