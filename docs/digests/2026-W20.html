Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Robot Learning: A Dexterous Manipulation Study on Reactive Catching

Kejia Ren, Gaotian Wang, Andrew S. Morgan et al.  ·  2026-05-10

Introduces Domain-Randomized Instance Set (DRIS) for zero-shot sim-to-real transfer via simultaneous multi-instance dynamics propagation.

ProblemConventional domain randomization randomizes dynamics once per episode, providing insufficient exposure to physical variability for high-sensitivity reactive tasks.ApproachDRIS propagates a set of randomized physical instances simultaneously during each step of the policy execution. This enables the policy to learn actions that are robust across a distribution of possible outcomes within a single trajectory.ResultAchieved reliable zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for reactive flat-plate catching using only 10 concurrent instances, maintaining robustness against perception noise.Why it mattersOffers a computationally efficient method to improve physical robustness in VLA and robot learning models without requiring extensive real-world fine-tuning.

PriorVLA: Prior-Preserving Adaptation for Vision-Language-Action Models

Xinyu Guo, Bin Xie, Wei Chai et al.  ·  2026-05-11

Preserves pretrained VLA priors during adaptation using a frozen prior expert and query-based distillation for improved generalization.

ProblemFull fine-tuning of VLAs shifts broad pretrained priors toward narrow downstream distributions, causing catastrophic forgetting and poor OOD generalization.ApproachFreezes the pretrained VLA as a Prior Expert and trains a specialized Adaptation Expert. Expert Queries extract scene and motor priors from the frozen model to guide the trainable expert, updating only 25% of parameters.ResultAchieves 99.1% success on LIBERO and outperforms pi0.5 by 22% in few-shot OOD real-world tasks with only 10 demonstrations.Why it mattersProvides a parameter-efficient fine-tuning strategy that maintains the zero-shot capabilities of foundation models while specializing for specific robotic embodiments.

RePO-VLA: Recovery-Driven Policy Optimization for Vision-Language-Action Models

Weijia Liufu, Xiaoyu Guo, Ruiyi Chen et al.  ·  2026-05-10

Optimizes VLA robustness by training on recovery-aware data and conditioning policies on a learned semantic value function.

ProblemSuccess-only imitation learning leaves VLAs unable to handle execution drift or terminal failures in contact-rich, long-horizon manipulation tasks.ApproachImplements Recovery-Aware Initialization to isolate corrective actions and a Progress-Aware Semantic Value Function to label trajectories via success-reference alignment. Value-Conditioned Refinement then trains the policy to prefer high-progress actions, biasing deployment toward the success manifold.ResultIncreased average adversarial success from 20% to 75%, with real-world bimanual task performance reaching 80%.Why it mattersEnables VLAs to recover from out-of-distribution states by salvaging failed rollouts, eliminating the need for explicit online failure detectors or heuristic retries.

Hand-in-the-Loop: Improving Dexterous VLA via Seamless Interventional Correction

Zhuohang Li, Liqun Huang, Wei Xu et al.  ·  2026-05-14

A human-in-the-loop method blending corrective intent with VLA execution to eliminate configuration jumps in dexterous manipulation.

ProblemHigh-DoF dexterous policies suffer from 'gesture jumps' during human intervention due to command mismatches between teleoperation and current state. This discontinuity causes grasp failures and disrupts contact-rich, long-horizon bimanual tasks.ApproachHandITL seamlessly blends human corrective inputs with autonomous policy signals rather than performing abrupt teleoperation takeovers. This integration preserves kinematic consistency and maintains stable contact dynamics during the transition between human and robot control.ResultThe method reduced takeover jitter by 99.8% and grasp failures by 87.5%, while refined policies outperformed standard teleoperation-based training by 19%.Why it mattersProvides a robust framework for Interactive Imitation Learning in high-DoF spaces where data quality depends on smooth transitions between human and policy. It enables more effective fine-tuning of VLA models for complex, bimanual dexterous tasks.

RotVLA: Rotational Latent Action for Vision-Language-Action Model

Qiwei Li, Xicheng Gong, Xinghang Li et al.  ·  2026-05-13

RotVLA uses continuous SO(n) rotational latent actions and flow-matching for structured, high-capacity cross-embodiment robot learning.

ProblemDiscrete quantization in current Latent Action Models causes representation degeneration, limited capacity, and fails to capture the continuous geometric nature of physical motion.ApproachActions are modeled as continuous elements of SO(n) using a triplet frame learning framework to enforce temporal dynamics. A flow-matching head decodes these rotational latents, which serve as a high-level planner for downstream action generation.ResultAchieved 98.2% on LIBERO and 89.6% on RoboTwin2.0 with 1.7B parameters, outperforming existing VLAs in both simulation and real-world manipulation.Why it mattersIntroduces a geometrically grounded alternative to VQ-based tokens, improving representational capacity and cross-embodiment transfer for robotic foundation models.

What to Ignore, What to React: Visually Robust RL Fine-Tuning of VLA Models

Yuanfang Peng, Jingjing Fu, Chuheng Zhang et al.  ·  2026-05-13

Enhances VLA robustness by supervising action distribution invariance and sensitivity across paired visual variants during RL fine-tuning.

ProblemStandard RL rewards fail to distinguish between task-irrelevant visual noise and changes requiring behavioral adaptation. This causes VLA policies to degrade under out-of-distribution visual shifts like new distractors or lighting.ApproachImplements auxiliary objectives during PPO that regularize action distributions using paired visual variants. An invariance term minimizes discrepancies for task-preserving shifts (e.g., distractors), while a sensitivity term ensures distinct distributions for task-altering shifts (e.g., object pose).ResultAchieved average success rate improvements of 16.62% on pi_0.5 and 9.10% on OpenVLA over baseline PPO across ManiSkill3 benchmarks.Why it mattersProvides a robust fine-tuning framework for deploying VLAs in visually dynamic environments. It demonstrates that structured behavior-level guidance on visual variants generalizes better than simple data augmentation or task rewards alone.

IntentVLA: Short-Horizon Intent Modeling for Aliased Robot Manipulation

Shijie Lian, Bin Yu, Xiaopeng Lin et al.  ·  2026-05-14

Stabilizes VLA policies by conditioning action generation on history-derived latent intent representations to resolve short-horizon observation aliasing.

ProblemFrame-conditioned VLAs suffer from inter-chunk conflict when identical visual-language observations map to different demonstration intents. This partial observability causes unstable execution as policies resample different modes during sequential replanning.ApproachIntegrates a history-conditioned encoder that compresses recent observations into a compact short-horizon intent representation. This representation conditions chunked action generation to ensure temporal consistency and modal alignment across inference steps.ResultImproves rollout stability and outperforms baselines on LIBERO, RoboCasa, and AliasBench, a new 12-task benchmark specifically designed to isolate observation aliasing.Why it mattersProvides a robust method for handling multimodal imitation data where history is essential for disambiguating task phases. It directly addresses the common 'jitter' and failure modes in chunked VLA deployments.
