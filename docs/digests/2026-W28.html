A progress-enhanced VLA for real-scale bimanual furniture assembly using joint action and progress signal prediction.

SA-VLA: State-aware tokenizer for improving Vision-Language-Action Models' performance

Tengyue Jiang, Chunpu Xu, Jiayue Kang et al.  ·  2026-06-29

SA-VLA introduces state-conditioned action tokenization using cross-attention or adapters to improve continuous action reconstruction accuracy for discrete VLA policies.

ProblemDiscrete action tokenizers typically map codes to static continuous prototypes, ignoring how robot proprioception influences required control signals. This fixed mapping creates a discretization gap that hinders precision in manipulation tasks where actions depend on current joint configurations.ApproachThe method conditions VQ-based action decoding on robot state using either cross-attention or a lightweight adapter that predicts state-dependent modulation factors. This allows each discrete token to represent a family of actions, effectively expanding codebook support without increasing the token vocabulary size.ResultSA-VLA increased average success rates from 0.29 to 0.56 on 12 RoboTwin tasks and from 0.15 to 0.33 in zero-shot sim-to-real experiments. These gains were achieved while maintaining compatibility with both autoregressive and parallel LLM-based VLA decoding.Why it mattersIt provides a robust mechanism to reduce quantization errors in VLA models, enabling higher precision and better sim-to-real transfer for complex manipulation tasks.

Actuator Reality Shaping for Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Robot Learning

Satoshi Yamamori, Koji Ishihara, Kentaro Minamikawa et al.  ·  2026-07-02

Forces physical actuator behavior to match idealized simulation dynamics using 2-DOF control for zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

ProblemDiscrepancies between ideal simulator actuator models and nonlinear, hardware-dependent physical motor dynamics lead to policy failure during deployment.ApproachImplements a two-degree-of-freedom feedforward-feedback controller to shape physical hardware behavior into prescribed second-order reference dynamics. This creates a standardized hardware interface that decouples robust stabilization from the reference-response expected by the policy.ResultEnabled zero-shot transfer on a 7-DOF arm, wheeled-legged robot, and humanoid walking, significantly reducing tracking errors compared to standard servo-control and real-to-sim-to-real baselines.Why it mattersProvides a hardware-agnostic abstraction layer that allows VLA and RL models to assume ideal dynamics, eliminating the need for complex actuator system identification.

ProblemInconsistent architectures and evaluation protocols prevent objective comparisons of VLA training paradigms. Action-only modeling specifically suffers from instability when trained on large-scale, heterogeneous robot datasets.ApproachUses a pi0-style flow-matching architecture to compare language-supervised co-training and world-model-style future latent alignment. Language supervision preserves vision-language generalization while future latent alignment improves state-transition and action-outcome modeling.Why it mattersProvides a standardized benchmark for VLA objectives and demonstrates that auxiliary tasks are necessary to stabilize training on diverse, multi-embodiment robot datasets.

Lightweight inference framework enabling adaptive action horizons via latent-space monitoring and event-triggered corrective replanning for VLAs.

Z-1: Efficient Reinforcement Learning for Vision-Language-Action Models

Lang Cao, Renhong Chen, Luyi Li et al.  ·  2026-06-30

Z-1 is an RL post-training framework using task-wise GRPO to enhance flow-based VLA models for robotic manipulation.

ProblemVLA models are typically limited by behavior cloning from fixed demonstrations, which prevents policies from learning from failures or exploring suboptimal states. This reliance on static datasets restricts the final performance and robustness of robot control.ApproachApplies task-wise Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to flow-based VLAs using shared-prefix rollouts and tree-structured trajectory branching. The method integrates completion-aware reward calibration and selective joint training of the Vision-Language Model and Action Expert components.ResultAchieved an 80.6% average success rate across 24 RoboCasa tasks, a 13.2% improvement over SFT baselines and a new state-of-the-art. Performance gains were achieved using only publicly available demonstration data.Why it mattersProvides a scalable methodology for applying RL post-training to large-scale robotic models, proving that GRPO can significantly refine VLA policies beyond supervised limits.

ProblemCurrent VLA models treat tactile signals as passive auxiliary inputs, failing to capture complex tactile semantics or predict future physical interaction dynamics.ApproachUses a unified tactile latent space with tactile chain-of-thought reasoning and coarse-to-fine future state prediction.
